## Summary

Comprehensive survey tracing anamorphosis from its Renaissance origins through to modern immersive perspective systems. Reformulates classical theory in a contemporary mathematical framework suitable for spherical perspectives and VR. Part of the Springer Handbook of the Mathematics of the Arts and Sciences.

## Key Findings

- Anamorphosis = a deliberately distorted image that resolves into a recognizable form only from a specific privileged viewpoint (or via a specific mirror)
- Historical types: plane (oblique viewing angle), catoptric (cylindrical/conical mirrors), and immersive (spherical perspectives)
- The mathematical core is a projective transformation between the "correct" image and the distorted version
- Modern extensions include VR/AR where viewpoint-dependence is natural

## Relevance to This Work

**Anamorphosis is the direct art-historical precedent for our work — and the source of the "anamorph" in our terminology.** The connections:

- Classical anamorphosis: privileged viewpoint in 3D physical space → our work: privileged subspace in $\mathbb{R}^d$
- Classical: the distorted image on a surface → our work: the point cloud in ambient space
- Classical: resolve via correct viewing angle or mirror → our work: resolve via correct projection or semantic key
- Classical: decoys are the distorted appearance from wrong viewpoints → our work: decoys are coherent-but-misleading forms from wrong subspaces

**The key difference:** classical anamorphosis operates in 3D Euclidean space with physical optics. Our latent anamorphs operate in high-dimensional abstract spaces where the "viewpoint" is a point on a Grassmannian and the "optics" are linear algebra. No prior work extends the anamorphic concept to high-dimensional latent spaces.

This paper is the best historical/mathematical survey to cite for establishing the lineage.
