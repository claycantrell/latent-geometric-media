## Summary

Introduces Concept Activation Vectors (CAVs) — linear classifiers trained in a neural network's activation space to distinguish concept examples from random examples. The normal vector of the learned decision boundary serves as the "concept direction." Directional derivatives along this direction quantify how sensitive a model's predictions are to a human-interpretable concept (e.g., "striped" for zebra classification).

## Key Findings

- Concepts correspond to linear directions in activation space
- A CAV is obtained by training a linear classifier on concept-positive vs. random examples at a chosen layer
- TCAV scores = directional derivatives along CAV direction, giving global class-level concept sensitivity
- Enables testing hypotheses about model behavior without per-instance attribution

## Methodology

- Collect examples of a concept (e.g., images with stripes) and random counterexamples
- Train a linear SVM in the activation space of a chosen layer
- The learned hyperplane normal = the CAV
- Compute directional derivatives of the model's output along the CAV to get TCAV scores

## Relevance to This Work

**TCAV is the foundation for our Type II (semantic-keyed) anamorphs.** In a Type II anamorph, the privileged projection basis is derived from concept directions rather than arbitrary coordinates. The connection:

- CAVs define meaningful linear directions in a representation space → our Type II uses such directions as the privileged subspace
- The concept direction is recoverable from examples + linear classification → our semantic clue channel provides concepts that define the projection
- TCAV demonstrates that "concepts = directions" is a viable and robust principle → we exploit this to make the privileged projection recoverable via semantic understanding

The key difference: TCAV *discovers* concept directions in learned representations. We *design* objects where the correct view is defined by concept directions.

## Questions & Follow-ups

- Can we use actual CAVs from a pretrained model as the basis for a Type II anamorph? (Yes — this is the Phase 2 prototype)
- How stable are CAVs across random seeds / example sets? Instability would make recovery unreliable.
